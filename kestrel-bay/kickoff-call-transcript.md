**You:** Dana, who do you buy from?

**Dana Okafor:** Frozen is mostly imported. Shrimp and pangasius come from Vietnam, salmon portions from Chile, and cod, haddock and snow crab from Canada. Pollock blocks come from Alaska and lobster tails from Maine. Fresh is local. We buy off the day boats every morning, but that's a separate thing. It's daily and I don't need help with it.

### **00:01:10**

**You:** Walk me through how you decide what to order.

**Dana Okafor:** Every Monday I pull the on-hand report and last month's sales from our system, which is called BlueTide and is ancient. I put both in Excel and go line by line. It takes half the day, because the reports don't agree with each other. Some things are in cases and some are in pounds, so I'm converting in my head.

**You:** What are you trying to find out?

**Dana Okafor:** Two things: what I'm going to run out of, and what I'm sitting on too much of.

**You:** Which one hurts more?

**Dana Okafor:** Running out. Last December we ran out of 16/20 shrimp ten days before Christmas. I saw it coming, but too late. Shrimp from Vietnam takes ten to twelve weeks door to door now.

**Marco Ruiz:** I had to call every account. I'd rather argue about price than tell a chef we're out.

### **00:02:25**

**You:** So the problem isn't knowing you're low. It's knowing early enough.

**Dana Okafor:** Right. Last month's sales tells me where I've been. It doesn't tell me that if I don't order this week, I'm out in March.

**You:** What are the lead times for each vendor?

**Dana Okafor:** Vietnam is ten to twelve weeks. It used to be eight, and the vendor sheet in BlueTide still says eight for Bien Xanh. Chile is about six weeks, and I don't think that one is even in the system. Canada is about two weeks. The day boats deliver tomorrow.

**Walt Brennan:** That's the theme. Half of what we actually know lives in Dana's head, not the system. If she's out for two weeks, I'm blind.

### **00:03:30**

**You:** What about sitting on too much?

**Dana Okafor:** Right now that's the Chilean salmon. I bought heavy over the summer because the price was good, and those lots are getting old. Frozen is good for eighteen to twenty-four months from the pack date, but nobody will take it with less than six months left. Once a lot is inside six months, I'm discounting it or dumping it. The expiration date is on every lot.

**Walt Brennan:** And it costs us money while it sits. Our building fills up, so overflow goes to Harbor Cold Storage, and they charge by the pallet every month. Long stock sitting at Harbor is the worst case.

**You:** How many locations is that?

**Dana Okafor:** Three: New Bedford, a smaller building in Providence, and Harbor. For ordering, I don't care which one stock is in. It's all mine, and we move it around. Harbor only matters because of the cost.

### **00:04:40**

**You:** Walt, what would success look like for you?

**Walt Brennan:** One screen on Monday morning that tells us what we need to order and what we're stuck with. And it has to be right. Nobody trusts the current reports because they count things that aren't really there.

**You:** Like what?

**Walt Brennan:** Stock on hold. The warehouse has two pretend locations, QA-HOLD and DAMAGED. QA-HOLD is waiting on paperwork or a temperature check. Sometimes it passes and comes back. DAMAGED gets written off eventually. Both show up as on hand. If I can't sell it, it isn't inventory.

**Dana Okafor:** And dead items. Anything starting with ZZ is from the old system, and we never deleted those. There's supposed to be a discontinued status, but people don't always set it. Some items we just stopped buying.

### **00:05:50**

**You:** On cases versus pounds, what's tracked in pounds?

**Dana Okafor:** Salmon sides, because they're catch weight and every side weighs something different. The bulk pollock blocks too. Everything else is cases, mostly. Sometimes a vendor ships a case item in pounds and the warehouse receives it that way. Each lot has a unit column that says CS or LB.

**You:** How do I convert cases to pounds?

**Dana Okafor:** The pack size is on the item, but it's whatever someone typed, like "10 lb cs" or "4x2.5lb". It's not a number.

**You:** Anything else odd about the on-hand export?

**Dana Okafor:** It runs nightly, and sometimes the file has two nights in it. There's a date column.

### **00:06:50**

**You:** Marco, what's weird about the sales data from your side?

**Marco Ruiz:** Three things. When I give a chef samples, I have to enter a zero-dollar order. When a chef sends something back, accounting enters a credit, which shows up as a negative sale. And BlueTide can't do transfers between buildings, so moving stock from New Bedford to Providence gets entered as a sale to a customer called "TRANSFER - PVD".

**Dana Okafor:** Which all ends up in my sales numbers. I think I filter the transfers out. Mostly.

**Marco Ruiz:** And last spring we had a cruise line order, about six weeks of shrimp in one go. They never came back. That was a one-off.

### **00:07:55**

**You:** Does demand change a lot through the year?

**Dana Okafor:** A lot. Lent is huge, from late February to Easter. Cod, haddock, pollock, anything white. Then the holidays are crab, shrimp and lobster tails. Summer is slower for frozen. So right now last month's sales is October, which is slow, and December won't be. I know that, so I adjust in my head.

### **00:08:30**

**You:** Once you know you need something, how do you decide how much to order?

**Dana Okafor:** Mostly gut. On imports I like about two weeks of extra stock on top, because ships slip. Canada, less. And I have to check open POs or I'll double order. I also check Marco's open orders, the ones booked but not shipped yet. That stock is already spoken for, even though it's still in the building.

**You:** How reliable is the PO report?

**Dana Okafor:** The quantities are right, the dates aren't. The arrival date is whatever the vendor said when I ordered, and nobody updates it. I have open POs that say they arrived three weeks ago but are still on the water. If a PO had really arrived, it would have been received and closed. Some arrive in part, say 600 of 1,000 cases, and the PO stays open for the other 400. The report also includes received and cancelled POs, so you have to filter those out.
